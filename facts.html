Planets Facts

    Planets

  • Earth: Earth is the only known planet with liquid water on its surface, essential for sustaining life. It orbits the Sun at an average distance of about 93 million miles, taking approximately 365.25 days to complete one orbit.
  • Mercury: is the closest planet to the Sun and has extreme temperature variations, ranging from -330°F at night to 800°F during the day. It has no atmosphere to retain heat and only takes about 88 Earth days to complete one orbit around the Sun.
  • Venus: has a thick, toxic atmosphere mostly made of carbon dioxide, which creates a runaway greenhouse effect, making it the hottest planet in our solar system with surface temperatures around 900°F. It rotates in the opposite direction to most planets, making its day longer than its year.
  • The Moon: is Earth's only natural satellite and is about 1/4th the size of Earth. It has no atmosphere, which leads to extreme temperature fluctuations, and its gravitational pull is responsible for creating tides on Earth.
  • Neptune: is the farthest planet from the Sun and has the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching speeds of over 1,200 miles per hour. Its deep blue color is due to methane in its atmosphere, which absorbs red light and reflects blue.
  • The Sun: is a massive ball of hot gas, primarily hydrogen and helium, and accounts for about 99.86% of the mass in our solar system. It produces energy through nuclear fusion, emitting light and heat that sustains life on Earth.
  • Jupiter: is the largest planet in our solar system, with a diameter over 11 times that of Earth. It has a Great Red Spot, a giant storm that has been raging for centuries, and a strong magnetic field that is the largest of any planet.
  • Saturn: Saturn is known for its stunning ring system, composed of ice, rock, and dust particles. It is the second-largest planet in the solar system and has 83 known moons, with Titan being the largest, even bigger than the planet Mercury.
